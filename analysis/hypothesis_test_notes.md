# Hypothesis Test Notes

## Objective

The objective of this experiment is to determine whether the new onboarding experience improves Paid Conversion Rate compared to the existing onboarding process.

---

## Business Decision

The business must decide whether the Treatment onboarding experience should be launched to all users.

This decision directly impacts:

* Revenue Growth
* Customer Acquisition
* Product Adoption
* Customer Experience

---

## Primary Metric Being Tested

### Paid Conversion Rate

Paid Conversion Rate was selected as the primary metric because it directly measures the percentage of users who become paying customers.

This metric has the strongest connection to business growth and recurring revenue.

---

## Null Hypothesis (H0)

The Treatment onboarding experience does not improve Paid Conversion Rate.

H0:

Treatment Conversion Rate ≤ Control Conversion Rate

---

## Alternative Hypothesis (H1)

The Treatment onboarding experience improves Paid Conversion Rate.

H1:

Treatment Conversion Rate > Control Conversion Rate

---

## Test Type

One-Tailed Hypothesis Test

A one-tailed test is appropriate because the experiment is specifically designed to determine whether the Treatment experience performs better than the Control experience.

---

## Significance Level

Alpha (α) = 0.05

Confidence Level = 95%

---

## Summary of Inputs

| Metric               | Control | Treatment |
| -------------------- | ------- | --------- |
| Users                | 693     | 715       |
| Paid Conversion Rate | 3.17%   | 6.99%     |

---

## Decision Rule

If p-value < 0.05:

Reject the Null Hypothesis.

If p-value ≥ 0.05:

Fail to Reject the Null Hypothesis.

---

## Interpretation Logic

A statistically significant increase in Paid Conversion Rate would indicate that the new onboarding experience successfully improves business performance.

The decision to launch should also consider guardrail metrics including:

* Refund Rate
* Support Ticket Rate
* Engagement Score

This ensures that conversion improvements do not create hidden operational or customer experience risks.

---

## Business Interpretation

The Treatment group achieved a substantially higher Paid Conversion Rate compared to the Control group.

The observed improvement supports the conclusion that the new onboarding experience contributes positively to business growth.

The experiment results provide evidence in favor of launching the Treatment onboarding experience while continuing to monitor guardrail metrics after deployment.

---

## Conclusion

Based on the experiment results, the evidence supports rejection of the Null Hypothesis and indicates that the Treatment onboarding experience improves Paid Conversion Rate.

This finding supports a recommendation to launch the new onboarding experience.
